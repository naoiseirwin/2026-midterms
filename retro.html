US 2026 Midterms: how the forecast did (provisional)

How our final pre-election forecast compares with the results counted so far and with other forecasters. Results as of 4 Nov, 5:33 PM ET; counting continues and 59 races are still uncalled, so every score here is provisional.

Democrats won both the House and the Senate. We gave Democrats a 94.0% chance in the House and 71.6% in the Senate. Democrats did better than we expected nearly everywhere: the national House vote stands at D+11.4 with about 89% of the expected vote counted, against our projection of D+8.7.

Calibration

All called races, grouped by our forecast chance. Dots on the dashed line would mean perfect calibration; bars show 90% intervals.

Races we rated as near-certain went as expected. In the middle, where we gave the Democratic side between 20% and 90%, it won 40 of 44 races against an average forecast of 58%: the forecast leaned too far toward Republicans.

Margins are provisional and include votes for minor-party and write-in candidates, which our two-candidate projections leave out, so some misses are overstated.

Alaska's at-large House seat uses ranked-choice voting. On first choices Nick Begich (R) leads by 9.2 points; we gave Bill Hill a 16.0% chance. It will be scored once the ranked-choice count is done.

Lessons

  • The national environment was stronger for Democrats than we projected, by about 2.8 points; that one miss accounts for about a quarter of the race-level error.
  • The misses all ran the same way: in competitive races, Democrats won more often than our chances implied.
  • Several Texas districts moved far more than the national swing, which points to a regional or demographic shift our district model did not capture.
  • Published race ratings did better than our district-by-district chances, which suggests our district forecasts needed more race-specific information.
  • Each chamber is a single outcome, so a better or worse chamber score for one forecaster says little about skill.
